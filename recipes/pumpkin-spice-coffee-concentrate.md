# Pumpkin Spice Coffee Concentrate

**Time:** 20 min active / 55 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ------------ | -------- | -------- | ------ |
| Butternut Squash | 350g | 2 1/2 cup cubes | 158 |
| Ground Cloves | 0.5g | 1/4 tsp | 1 |
| Water | 20g | 1 1/2 tbsp | 0 |
| Coconut Milk | 50g | 3 1/2 tbsp | 98 |
| Ground Cinnamon | 5g | 2 tsp | 12 |
| Ground Allspice | 1.5g | 3/4 tsp | 4 |
| Fresh Ginger | 12g | 2 tbsp | 10 |
| Maple Syrup | 80g | 1/4 cup | 208 |
| Coconut Sugar | 40g | 3 1/2 tbsp | 152 |
| Lemon Juice | 8g | 1 1/2 tsp | 2 |
| Vanilla Extract | 6g | 1 1/2 tsp | 17 |
| Nutmeg | 0.6g | 1/4 tsp | 3 |

**Total:** 665 kcal | 5g protein

## Steps

1. Cut the squash into chunks and lay them cut-side down on a lined tray. Roast hot until the flesh has fully collapsed and the undersides are deep caramel-brown, about 35 minutes -- that hard browning concentrates the squash's sugars into real pumpkin-pie depth, not watery, bland flesh.
2. While it roasts, dry-toast the whole cloves in a small pan over medium heat, shaking, just until fragrant, about a minute; cool, then grind to a fine powder. Pre-ground clove is already half-flat; toasting and grinding fresh wakes the oils.
3. Scoop the roasted flesh away from the skins into a blender, add the water, and blend completely smooth -- no fibres, so it suspends cleanly in coffee.
4. Warm the coconut milk in a pan over low heat and stir in the ground cloves, cinnamon, and allspice. Let them sizzle gently until fragrant, 1 to 2 minutes. This bloom is the key step: these spices' aromas are fat-soluble and barely dissolve in water, so the coconut fat is what actually pulls them out and carries them through.
5. Stir in the squash puree, grated ginger, maple syrup, coconut sugar, lemon, and a small pinch of salt. Simmer gently, stirring so it never catches, until thick and glossy and reduced by about a third, around 12 minutes. The slow simmer is deliberate -- it converts the ginger's raw, sharp bite into sweet, mellow warmth.
6. Off the heat, stir in the vanilla and grate the nutmeg in fresh. Both are volatile and a hot simmer would cook them flat, so they go in last. Cool completely before storing.
7. Pack into a clean jar for the fridge, or spoon into an ice-cube tray and freeze, then bag the cubes. Stir a spoonful, or drop a frozen cube, into hot coffee; grate a little extra nutmeg over the cup.

## Notes

The flavor design here is built on one piece of spice science: the compounds we want -- eugenol from clove and allspice, cinnamaldehyde from cinnamon, the warm gingerols, the bright terpenes of nutmeg -- are nearly insoluble in water. A plain water-and-squash syrup leaves them stranded in the pulp, tasting thin. Blooming the ground spices in a little coconut fat first is what unlocks them; the spoonful of coconut milk is a flavor carrier, not enough to make this a creamer. For the cinnamon, use a roughly 2:1 blend of your Ceylon and Cassia -- the Cassia brings punch, the Ceylon floral delicacy without the coumarin load. Clove is kept deliberately tiny; it tips from warm to numbing fast. The ginger is cooked in on purpose rather than added fresh, because a gentle simmer mellows its sharpness; the nutmeg and vanilla, the opposite, go in off the heat to keep their top notes alive -- and a fresh grate of nutmeg over the finished cup is where they sing loudest.

Storage matters: squash puree is a LOW-ACID food, so this is a perishable condiment, NOT a shelf-stable syrup. Keep it in the fridge no more than a week, or -- better -- freeze it as ice cubes and keep them for months, thawing one coffee's worth at a time. Discard at any sign of mould, off smell, or sliminess. Stir or froth it well in the cup; for a pumpkin-spice latte, top with hot milk.
