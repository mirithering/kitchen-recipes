# Lemon Rice with Aubergine Coconut Dal

**Time:** 30 min active / 40 min total

## Steps

1. Heat the oven to 220C. Cut the aubergine into big skin-on cubes and the broccoli into florets, toss so every face is coated in the roasting oil, and spread in a single uncrowded layer. Roast about 25 minutes, turning once, until the aubergine is dark golden and collapsing-soft and the broccoli edges are charred.
2. Rinse the red lentils until the water runs clear, then simmer in salted water, skimming the grey foam in the first few minutes, for about 20 minutes until they slump into a loose puree. Keep them soupy -- add a splash of water if they tighten.
3. Rinse the basmati until the water runs clear, boil in plenty of water for about 10 minutes until just tender, drain well, and leave covered under a clean towel to steam-dry so the grains stay separate.
4. For the dal base, fry the shallots in the oil over medium heat, stirring often, for about 8 minutes until soft and dark golden. Stir in the garlic, ginger, and dried chili for a minute until fragrant, then add the cumin seeds, ground coriander, turmeric, and asafoetida and toast for 30 seconds, just until the spices smell nutty, before they catch.
5. Stir the lentils into the spiced base, loosening with a little of their cooking water to a spoonable sauce, then fold in the roasted aubergine and broccoli.
6. Pour in the coconut milk and bring to a bare simmer -- never a rolling boil, or it splits -- for a minute or two. Off the heat, stir through the lemon juice and taste for salt.
7. For the rice, heat the tadka oil until it shimmers, add the mustard seeds and wait for them to crackle and pop, then the cashews, frying until golden. Drop in the curry leaves and dried chili to splutter, and finally the asafoetida and turmeric -- then pull the pan off the heat at once so nothing scorches. Fold the tempered oil through the warm rice with the lemon juice and salt.
8. Pile the lemon rice on the plate, spoon the dal alongside, and finish the dal with a thread of raw oil.

## Notes

This is two South Indian ideas on one plate: chitranna (lemon rice) and a saucy roasted-aubergine dal, tied together by coconut. The dal leans on red lentils because they're the mildest, sweetest lentil -- hulled, so there's no bitter seed-coat tannin -- and they collapse into a silky sauce. Rinse them till the water runs clear and skim the early foam and any muddiness is gone. Salt goes in the cooking water, not at the end: lentils salted from within taste seasoned all the way through, where a dal salted late tastes flat with a salty edge.

Two things make or break it. Coconut milk splits above about 80C, turning grainy and oily, so it goes in last at a bare simmer -- never a hard boil. And the tadka is a race against burning: mustard seeds first (they need the heat to pop), curry leaves and chili next, asafoetida last, because hing chars to permanent bitterness in seconds. The moment it hits the oil, the pan comes off the heat. Roast the aubergine hot and uncrowded with the oil brushed over it, not pooled under it -- big cubes in a single layer collapse their air pockets and go silky; a cool or crowded tray steams them into oily sponge. Lemon always off the heat, in both rice and dal, to keep it bright.
