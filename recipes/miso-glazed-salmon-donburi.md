# Miso-Glazed Salmon Donburi

**Time:** 20 min active / 40 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume     | Kcal | Step |
|------------|--------|------------|------|------|
| Salmon       | 140g | 1 fillet   | 183 | 1 |
| White Rice   |  75g | 1/3 cup    | 270 | 2 |
| Water        |  85g | 1/3 cup    |   0 | 3 |
| Red Cabbage  |  80g | 1 cup      |  25 | 4 |
| Lime Juice   |  15g | 1 tbsp     |   4 | 4 |
| Sesame Oil   |   5g | 1 tsp      |  44 | 4 |
| Red Miso     |  15g | 2 3/4 tsp  |  30 | 5 |
| Maple Syrup  |  10g | 1 1/2 tsp  |  26 | 5 |
| Fresh Ginger |   5g | 2 1/2 tsp  |   4 | 5 |
| Dried Chili  | 0.5g | 1/4 tsp    |   2 | 5 |

**Total:** 588 kcal | 39g protein

## Steps

1. Submerge the sealed-frozen salmon in a bowl of cold water for about 15 minutes until fully pliable, then unwrap and pat very dry on both sides -- a dry surface is what lets the glaze caramelize instead of steam.
2. Rinse the rice in a bowl under cold water, swirling and pouring off the cloudy water 4-5 times until it runs nearly clear; this strips the surface starch that turns pot rice gummy.
3. Drain the rice, add the water to a small heavy pot, and let it soak 10 minutes; then bring to a boil over high heat, drop to the lowest possible simmer, clamp the lid on, and cook 13 minutes -- do not lift the lid even once -- then pull off the heat and rest, still covered, 10 minutes before fluffing.
4. While the rice cooks, shred the red cabbage as finely as you can; sprinkle with a little salt and scrunch hard with your hands for a minute until it wilts and beads with liquid, then toss with the lime juice and sesame oil and leave to sharpen.
5. Whisk the red miso, maple syrup, finely grated ginger, and dried chili into a smooth, glossy paste; smear it over the top of the salmon and broil 4-6 cm from the element for 4-6 minutes, watching closely, until the lacquer bubbles and just blackens at the edges and the fish flakes at a gentle press.
6. Fluff the rested rice into a bowl, lay the salmon on top, spoon over any glaze from the pan, and pile the bright cabbage alongside.

## Notes

Saikyo-yaki -- Kyoto's sweet-miso fish -- works because miso's amino acids and the maple's sugars brown fast and hard; that speed is also the trap, so the glaze goes on at the end and broils only minutes, not a long bake. The cold-water rest after the rice cooks is non-negotiable: the grains finish cooking in their own trapped steam and firm up, and every peek that vents that steam is why pot rice comes out crunchy or mushy. Salting and scrunching the cabbage draws out water and tames its raw bite in seconds, so it stays crisp and acidic against the rich fish rather than harshly raw.
