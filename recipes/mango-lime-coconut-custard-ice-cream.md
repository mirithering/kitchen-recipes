# Mango-Lime Coconut Custard Ice Cream

**Time:** 35 min active / 480 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ------------- | -------- | ------------- | ------ |
| Frozen Mango | 400g | 2 1/2 cup | 240 |
| Coconut Milk | 200g | 1/2 can | 394 |
| Inulin | 12g | 1 1/2 tbsp | 18 |
| Lime Zest | 4g | 2 1/4 tsp | 4 |
| Salt | 1g | pinch | 0 |
| Egg Yolk | 85g | 5 large | 274 |
| Sugar | 50g | 4 tbsp | 194 |
| Lime Juice | 30g | 2 tbsp | 8 |

**Total:** 1132 kcal \| 21g protein

## Steps

1. Blend the frozen mango to a smooth puree and press it through a fine sieve to catch the stringy fibers, then simmer the strained puree in a wide pan over medium-low, stirring often, until reduced by about half and thick enough to mound on the spoon, about 12-15 min. Scrape into a bowl.
2. Warm the coconut milk with the inulin, salt, and lime zest in a saucepan over medium, stirring, until steaming and the inulin has fully dissolved; do not let it boil.
3. Whisk the egg yolks with the sugar until pale and thick, then temper by whisking in the hot coconut milk in a thin stream; return to low heat and stir constantly with a spatula until it thickens into a custard that coats the back of the spoon, 77-82C.
4. Strain the custard through a sieve onto the mango and whisk smooth, then chill over an ice bath and refrigerate at least 4 hours or overnight until very cold.
5. Stir the lime juice into the cold base and taste for brightness.
6. Churn in the ice cream maker until thick and soft-serve, about 20-25 min.
7. Pack into a container, press parchment onto the surface, and freeze until firm, 3-4 hours.

## Notes

Frozen mango is over 80% water, and water is what makes ice cream icy -- simmering the puree down concentrates the flavor and drives off the moisture that would otherwise freeze into coarse crystals. Do not skip pressing the blended mango through a sieve first: mango is threaded with fine, tough fibers that never soften and would streak an otherwise silky scoop, so the sieve is what makes the difference between velvety and stringy. The egg-yolk custard, cooked to the nappe stage (77-82C), emulsifies the fat and water into a silky, scoopable set; straining catches any strands that overcook. Inulin (a chicory-root fiber) adds body and keeps the crystals small, so the sugar can stay modest; it is a prebiotic, so the dose is kept gentle. Coconut fat freezes firmer than dairy cream, giving a clean, dense scoop. The lime juice goes in cold on purpose -- stirred into the hot custard its acid would curdle the yolks; added at the end it lifts the mango and cuts the coconut richness. Let the tub soften a few minutes at room temperature before scooping.
