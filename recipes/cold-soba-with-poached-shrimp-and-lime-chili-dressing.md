# Cold Soba with Poached Shrimp and Lime-Chili Dressing

**Time:** 25 min active / 30 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| -------------------- | -------- | ---------------- | ------ |
| Cucumber | 100g | 1 cup sliced | 15 |
| Red Cabbage | 70g | 1 cup shredded | 22 |
| Garlic | 6g | 2 clove | 9 |
| Ginger | 8g | 1 1/2 tsp grated | 6 |
| Dried Chili | 1g | 1/2 tsp flake | 3 |
| Lime | 67g | 1 fruit | 20 |
| Fish Sauce | 15g | 3/4 tbsp | 5 |
| Honey | 7g | 1 tsp | 21 |
| Shrimp | 130g | 10 medium | 110 |
| Soba Noodles | 80g | 1 thumb bundle | 269 |
| Black Sesame Seeds | 5g | 1 3/4 tsp | 29 |
| Sesame Oil | 5g | 1 tsp | 44 |

**Total:** 553 kcal | 42g protein

## Steps

1. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil.
2. While it heats, slice the cucumber into thin half-moons and shave the red cabbage as finely as you can; keep both cold.
3. Pound the garlic, ginger, and dried chili to a rough paste in a mortar, then stir in the lime juice, fish sauce, honey, and a splash of water until the honey dissolves into a sharp, salty, barely-sweet dressing.
4. Drop the shrimp into the boiling water, immediately turn off the heat, and poach gently for about 2 minutes until just pink and opaque through; lift straight into ice water, then drain and pat dry.
5. Return the water to a boil, add the soba, and cook for about 4-5 minutes until tender with no hard core, stirring now and then; drain and rinse hard under cold running water for 30 seconds, rubbing the strands to strip the surface starch, then drain very well.
6. Toss the soba with a spoonful of the dressing, pile into a bowl with the cucumber, cabbage, and shrimp, spoon over the rest of the dressing, scatter with black sesame, and finish with a thread of sesame oil.

## Notes

Two things keep this clean and bright instead of heavy. The soba: 100 percent buckwheat noodles shed a lot of starch, so the hard cold rinse after boiling -- actually rubbing the strands under the tap -- is what stops them gluing into a gummy clump. Skip it and the bowl turns pasty. The shrimp: dropping them into water you then take off the heat poaches them in cooling water rather than a hard boil, so they stay sweet and plump; a rolling boil seizes the proteins and they go rubbery. The ice bath locks that in.

The dressing is deliberately water-thinned and acid-led -- roughly equal lime and fish sauce, loosened with water and just enough honey to round the edges. That dilution is the point: undiluted fish sauce bullies everything, while the splash of water lets the lime and chili read. No oil in the dressing at all; the only fat is the thread of sesame oil at the end, there for aroma, not richness.
