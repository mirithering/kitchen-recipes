# Cold Wood Ear and Smashed Cucumber with Poached Shrimp

**Time:** 15 min active / 30 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient        | Weight | Volume     | Kcal | Step |
|-------------------|--------|------------|------|------|
| Wood Ear (dried)  |  14g   | 1/2 cup    |  40  | 1    |
| Cucumber          | 200g   | 2 cup      |  30  | 2    |
| Cooked Shrimp     | 120g   | 15 shrimp  | 119  | 4    |
| Garlic            |   6g   | 2 cloves   |   9  | 5    |
| Fresh Ginger      |  12g   | 2 tbsp     |  10  | 5    |
| Dried Chili       |   1g   | 1 tsp      |   3  | 5    |
| Rapeseed Oil      |   7g   | 1 1/2 tsp  |  62  | 5    |
| Peanut Butter     |  30g   | 2 tbsp     | 177  | 6    |
| Rice Vinegar      |  15g   | 1 tbsp     |   3  | 6    |
| Soy Sauce         |  12g   | 2 1/4 tsp  |   6  | 6    |
| Sugar             |   4g   | 1 tsp      |  15  | 6    |
| Sesame Oil        |   5g   | 1 tsp      |  44  | 6    |
| Black Sesame Seeds|   8g   | 2 3/4 tsp  |  46  | 7    |

**Total:** 564 kcal | 42g protein

## Steps

1. Rinse the wood ear and soak it in cool water for about 20 minutes, until the pieces unfurl completely and feel soft and pliable; lift out and drain. Do not leave it soaking at room temperature for hours -- rehydrated wood ear breeds bacteria fast.
2. Meanwhile, smash the cucumber with the flat of a knife until it cracks open, tear into rough bite-size chunks, toss with salt in a colander, and leave 15 minutes to weep; then squeeze it firmly in your hands until no more water runs out.
3. Blanch the drained wood ear in gently boiling water for 1-2 minutes, until tender with a faint spring, then plunge into ice water, drain well, and tear any large pieces to bite-size.
4. Thaw the shrimp under cold running water until just soft and pat it thoroughly dry; it is already cooked and needs no further heat, only chilling.
5. Mince the garlic and finely grate the ginger into a small heatproof bowl. Heat the rapeseed oil with the chili flakes until shimmering and fragrant, about 1 minute, then pour it sizzling over the garlic and ginger so it foams and blooms.
6. Whisk the peanut butter, rice vinegar, soy sauce, sugar and sesame oil into the bloomed aromatics, loosening with a splash of cool water until it pours like thin cream.
7. Toss the wood ear, cucumber and shrimp with the dressing until everything is glossed, then pile onto a plate and scatter with the toasted black sesame.

## Notes

This is a Northern Chinese cold-table classic (liang ban), where wood ear contributes almost no flavor but all of the pleasure: a cool, gelatinous snap against the juicy cucumber. Two moves make or break it. First, the cucumber must be salted and wrung dry -- the salt draws out the water that would otherwise dilute the dressing into a puddle, and smashing rather than slicing leaves torn faces that grip the sauce. Second, the wood ear gets a short blanch after soaking: it sets the springy texture and washes off the raw, earthy edge.

The dressing is built the traditional way, by pouring smoking-hot oil over raw garlic and ginger. The heat blooms the chili and rounds off the harsh raw bite, leaving the garlic mellow and the ginger warm rather than sharp. Real Northern cooks would use sesame paste (ma jiang); smooth peanut butter stands in beautifully and is just as authentic to the cold-noodle tradition, with sesame oil carrying the roasted note no paste is needed for.

A note on the wood ear: only ever soak it in cool water and use it the same day. Long warm soaks are the one genuine hazard with this mushroom.
