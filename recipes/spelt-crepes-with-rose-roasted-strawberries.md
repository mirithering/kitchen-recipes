# Spelt Crepes with Rose-Roasted Strawberries

**Time:** 20 min active / 30 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| --------------------- | -------- | ----------- | ------ |
| Spelt Flour | 45g | 1/3 cup | 152 |
| Manioc Starch | 8g | 2 3/4 tsp | 29 |
| Egg | 50g | 1 large | 72 |
| Oat Milk | 130g | 1/2 cup | 58 |
| Salt | 1g | pinch | 0 |
| Frozen Strawberries | 140g | 3/4 cup | 49 |
| Honey | 8g | 1 1/4 tsp | 24 |
| Rose Water | 1g | 1/4 tsp | 0 |
| Skyr | 150g | 2/3 cup | 76 |
| Honey | 7g | 1 tsp | 21 |
| Vanilla Extract | 2g | 1/2 tsp | 0 |
| Rapeseed Oil | 5g | 1 tsp | 44 |
| Pistachios | 15g | 2 tbsp | 84 |

**Total:** 609 kcal \| 31g protein

## Steps

1. Heat the oven to 220C. Whisk the spelt flour, manioc starch, and salt in a bowl, then whisk in the egg and oat milk until just smooth -- stop the moment the lumps disappear; overworking builds gluten and turns the crepes rubbery. Leave the batter to rest while the strawberries roast (the bran needs time to drink up the liquid).
2. Toss the strawberries with the honey in a small baking dish and roast for 20-25 min, stirring once halfway, until collapsed, glossy, and jammy with the watery edge cooked off. Stir the rose water in off the heat.
3. Stir the skyr with the honey and vanilla until smooth and glossy, then keep it cold -- it should stay thick and tangy.
4. Heat a nonstick pan over medium-high and wipe it with a film of the oil. Pour in a thin ladle of batter and swirl immediately to coat the pan; cook 1-2 min until the edge lifts and the underside is lacy-golden, then flip and cook 30-60 sec more. Repeat with the rest, stacking the crepes.
5. Smear each crepe with the skyr and fold into quarters. Spoon the roasted strawberries and their syrup over the top and scatter with the crushed pistachios.

## Notes

Frozen strawberries usually disappoint because they thaw to a pale, watery puddle. Roasting from frozen fixes this: the high oven drives off the released water and concentrates what's left into a deep, jammy syrup -- more strawberry than the fresh berry, not less. Rose water is volatile and turns soapy if you cook it or overdose it, so it goes in off the heat in a whisper -- just enough to lift the fruit, never enough to announce itself.

The crepe batter rests for a reason. Wholemeal spelt is full of thirsty bran; the rest lets it fully hydrate and lets the gluten relax, so the crepes cook up tender and pliable instead of tight and rubbery. The spoon of manioc starch helps too -- it dilutes the gluten and sets into a soft gel, keeping the crepe supple. The skyr stays unwhipped and barely sweet on purpose: its cold tang is the counterweight that keeps the plate from tipping into cloying, the way creme fraiche cuts a sweet tart. Pistachios instead of the usual almonds -- their green, resinous bite belongs with rose and strawberry.
