# Seared Halloumi with Roasted Beets, Orange and Hazelnut Dukkah

**Time:** 30 min active / 70 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal | Step |
|------------|--------|------------|------|------|
| Beetroot         | 164g | 2 beet     |  71 | 1 |
| Hazelnuts        |  15g | 2 tbsp     |  94 | 2 |
| Coriander seeds  |   1g | pinch      |   3 | 2 |
| Cumin seeds      |   1g | pinch      |   4 | 2 |
| Fennel seeds     |   1g | pinch      |   3 | 2 |
| Sesame seeds     |   5g | 1 3/4 tsp  |  29 | 2 |
| Halloumi         | 100g | 4 slice    | 330 | 4 |
| Skyr             | 100g | 1/3 cup    |  51 | 5 |
| Lemon            |  10g | 2 1/4 tsp  |   3 | 5 |
| Olive Oil        |   5g | 1 tsp      |  44 | 6 |
| Balsamic Vinegar |   5g | 1 tsp      |   4 | 6 |
| Honey            |   5g | 3/4 tsp    |  15 | 6 |
| Sumac            |   1g | pinch      |   3 | 6 |
| Orange           |  80g | 1/2 orange |  50 | 6 |
| Olive Oil        |   4g | 1 tsp      |  35 | 7 |
| Mint             |   2g | to taste   |   1 | 8 |

**Total:** 740 kcal | 38g protein

## Steps

1. Heat the oven to 220C. Wrap each beet tightly in foil and roast on a tray until a knife slides into the center with only faint resistance, about 1 hour. Let cool until you can handle them, then rub the skins off with a paper towel and cut into wedges.
2. In a dry pan over medium heat, toast the hazelnuts, shaking often, for 6-8 minutes until the skins darken and they smell deeply nutty; tip onto a towel and rub off the loose skins. Add the coriander, cumin, fennel, and sesame seeds to the same pan and toast for 1-2 minutes, tossing constantly, until the sesame turns pale gold and the spices are fragrant. Let everything cool completely.
3. Pulse the cooled nuts and seeds in a food processor in short bursts -- or pound in a mortar -- to a coarse, gravelly crumb, stopping while the pieces are still distinct so the hazelnut oil never smears it to paste. Season with a little salt.
4. Slice the halloumi about 1cm thick and soak in just-boiled water for 15 minutes to mellow the salt and squeak, then pat completely dry -- a wet surface won't crust.
5. Whisk the skyr with the lemon zest, a squeeze of lemon juice, and a pinch of salt until loose and spoonable; spread it across the base of the plate in a wide swoosh.
6. Toss the warm beet wedges with the olive oil, balsamic, honey, and sumac until glossy. Cut the peel from the orange and slice the segments free from their membranes.
7. Heat a non-stick pan over medium-high with the olive oil. Sear the halloumi for 2-3 minutes per side until deep golden and crisp-edged, and serve at once -- it firms as it cools.
8. Pile the dressed beets and orange over the skyr, lay the seared halloumi alongside, and shower with the dukkah and torn mint.

## Notes

Three textures, one plate: squeaky-seared cheese, silky beet, gravelly dukkah. Roasting the beets whole in foil traps their steam so the sugars concentrate instead of bleeding out -- the skins slip off once cooled, and the flesh stays jammy rather than dry. Halloumi won't melt (its curd is heated and brined, so the proteins set rather than flow), but a short soak in hot water draws out surface salt and relaxes that rubbery squeak; sear it hot and fast, because past three minutes a side the proteins tighten and toughen. The dukkah lives or dies on grinding cold -- warm hazelnuts smear into butter, so toast, cool fully, then pulse only to gravel. The cool lemony skyr underneath is the counterweight to the salty cheese and sweet beet; mint and orange keep it bright.
