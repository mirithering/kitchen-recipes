# Burmese Shan Tofu Salad

**Time:** 35 min active / 80 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ------------ | -------- | -------- | ------ |
| Chickpea flour | 90g | 1 cup | 348 |
| Water | 590g | 2 1/2 cup | 0 |
| Salt | 2g | 1/4 tsp | 0 |
| Rapeseed oil | 9g | 2 tsp | 80 |
| Garlic | 9g | 3 cloves | 13 |
| Dried chili | 1g | 1/2 tsp | 3 |
| Chickpea flour | 10g | 1 1/2 tbsp | 39 |
| Fish sauce | 12g | 2 tsp | 4 |
| Lime | 30g | 1/2 fruit | 9 |
| Caster sugar | 2g | 1/2 tsp | 8 |
| Red cabbage | 120g | 1 1/2 cup | 37 |
| Cherry tomato | 80g | 1/2 cup | 14 |
| Rapeseed oil | 9g | 2 tsp | 80 |

**Total:** 635 kcal \| 26g protein

## Steps

1. Whisk the chickpea flour, water, and salt in a saucepan until completely smooth, then let it sit a few minutes to hydrate -- whisking into cold water, never hot, is what keeps it lump-free.
2. Cook over medium heat, stirring and scraping the bottom constantly, for 8-10 minutes until the paste turns glossy, very thick, and pulls away from the sides in a single mass; scrape at once into a small nonstick or cling-film-lined dish, smooth the top flat, and leave to set firm, at least 1 hour.
3. Meanwhile make the chili-garlic oil: warm the oil with the thinly sliced garlic in a small pan over medium-low heat, stirring, for 3-4 minutes until the garlic is just pale blond -- lift it out onto paper while still light, as it keeps browning off the heat and crisps as it cools, then stir the dried chili into the warm oil off the heat and let it steep to a fragrant rust-red.
4. Dry-toast the chickpea flour in a small dry pan over medium heat, stirring without stopping, for 2-3 minutes until it smells nutty and turns the colour of almond skin -- tip it straight onto a plate the moment it colours, as it scorches in seconds.
5. Whisk the toasted flour, fish sauce, lime juice, and sugar with the chili-garlic oil into a loose, pourable dressing; taste and nudge it until salty, sour, and warm sit in balance.
6. Shred the red cabbage as finely as you can and halve the cherry tomatoes.
7. Turn the set tofu out and cut into thick batons. Heat the oil in a nonstick pan over medium-high until it shimmers, lay the batons in, and fry undisturbed 3-4 minutes a side until a deep golden crust forms and lets go of the pan on its own -- moving them early is what tears them.
8. Pile the warm tofu, cabbage, and tomatoes in a wide bowl, pour over the dressing, toss, scatter the crisp fried garlic on top, and eat at once while the tofu is crisp.

## Notes

Shan tofu (tohu) is the Burmese tofu with no soy in it at all -- it sets from chickpea flour alone, the way polenta sets from corn. Heat and patience make or break it: whisk the flour into cold water so the starch hydrates evenly (dropped into hot water it seizes into lumps), then cook it long enough that it turns glossy and pulls from the pan. Under-cooked, it stays chalky, tastes of raw bean, and never firms up. Once set and cooled it fries beautifully -- custardy inside a crackly crust -- as long as you leave it alone until the crust releases itself. The dressing is pure thoke logic: toasted chickpea flour thickens and deepens it, fried garlic lends crunch while its oil lends perfume, and fish sauce and lime are tuned against a whisper of sugar until nothing shouts. The cabbage and tomato aren't a garnish -- their cool crunch and juice are what the warm, rich tofu is built to land on.
