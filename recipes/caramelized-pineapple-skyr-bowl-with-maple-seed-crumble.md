# Caramelized Pineapple Skyr Bowl with Maple Seed Crumble

**Time:** 20 min active / 35 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient       | Weight | Volume      | Kcal | Step |
|------------------|--------|-------------|------|------|
| Frozen pineapple | 180g   | 3/4 cup     | 155  | 1    |
| Frozen mango     |  60g   | 1/3 cup     |  36  | 1    |
| Pumpkin seeds    |  12g   | 1 1/2 tbsp  |  67  | 2    |
| Sunflower seeds  |  18g   | 2 tbsp      | 105  | 2    |
| Sesame seeds     |   8g   | 1 tbsp      |  46  | 2    |
| Maple syrup      |   8g   | 1/2 tbsp    |  21  | 2    |
| Maple syrup      |  14g   | 3/4 tbsp    |  36  | 3    |
| Lime             |  30g   | 1/2 fruit   |   9  | 3    |
| Skyr             | 250g   | 1 cup       | 128  | 4    |

**Total:** 603 kcal | 33g protein

## Steps

1. Tip the frozen pineapple and mango into a sieve set over a bowl and leave to thaw, about 15 minutes, then press gently to drain off the released juice -- draining is what lets the fruit caramelize later instead of stewing in its own water.
2. In a dry pan over medium heat, toast the pumpkin seeds for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly, then add the sunflower seeds, and finally the sesame -- they scorch fastest. Once everything smells nutty and just starts to color, pour in the maple and stir 1-2 minutes until it thickens and coats the seeds; tip onto parchment, spread thin, and leave to crisp as it cools.
3. Wipe the pan and set over medium-high heat. Add the drained fruit and the maple and cook, stirring often, for 8-10 minutes until the juices reduce to a glossy amber syrup that coats the back of a spoon. Take off the heat and stir in the lime juice.
4. Whisk the skyr in a bowl just until smooth and glossy -- a gentle hand, not hard beating, which makes fat-free skyr weep; loosen with a teaspoon of water only if it is too stiff to spoon.
5. Spoon the skyr into a bowl, pile the warm caramelized fruit and its syrup over one side, scatter the seed crumble, and finish with a fine grating of lime zest.

## Notes

Freezing is the trick here, not a compromise: ice crystals rupture the fruit's cell walls, so on thawing it sheds juice and then caramelizes far faster than fresh -- you get a real glossy syrup in minutes. Draining the thaw-water first is essential; left in, it just simmers and the sugars never concentrate. Maple goes in early to drive browning, lime goes in last, off the heat, so its brightness survives.

Fat-free skyr carries the protein but has no fat to cushion its protein network, so it weeps and turns grainy if beaten hard. Whisk it just to smooth, no more. The hot syrup against the cool tart cream is the whole point -- don't let it sit.

Seeds burn in order of size: sesame first, then sunflower, then pumpkin, which is why they go in reverse. The maple is added only once they smell nutty; on a too-hot pan it seizes and scorches. Spread thin, it crisps as it cools into shards rather than a sticky clump.
