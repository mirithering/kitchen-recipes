# Tofu Tinga with Pickled Red Onion

**Time:** 25 min active / 30 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ------------ | -------- | -------- | ------ |
| Red onion (pickle) | 40g | 1/4 small | 16 |
| Apple cider vinegar | 15g | 1 tbsp | 3 |
| White rice | 70g | 1/3 cup | 252 |
| Salt |  | to taste | 0 |
| Firm tofu | 200g | 1 block | 170 |
| Olive oil | 15g | 1 tbsp | 133 |
| Manioc starch | 8g | 1 tbsp | 29 |
| Red onion (sauce) | 70g | 3/4 small | 28 |
| Garlic | 9g | 3 cloves | 13 |
| Sweet paprika | 2g | 1 tsp | 6 |
| Ground cumin | 2g | 1 tsp | 8 |
| Dried oregano | 1g | 3/4 tsp | 3 |
| Dried chili | 1g | 3/4 tsp | 3 |
| Chopped canned tomatoes | 200g | 3/4 cup | 64 |
| Smoked sriracha | 10g | 1 3/4 tsp | 9 |
| Sugar | 2g | 1/2 tsp | 8 |
| Lime juice | 15g | 1 tbsp | 4 |

**Total:** 749 kcal \| 33g protein

## Steps

1. Thinly slice the red onion for the pickle and pack it into a small bowl. Warm the vinegar with a splash of water and a pinch of salt until steaming, pour it over to submerge the slices, and press them under the liquid. Leave to mellow for the whole cook -- a short soak won't tame the raw bite, so give it the full window.
2. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then simmer it covered in salted water over low heat for about 12-15 minutes, until tender and the water is absorbed. Leave covered off the heat to steam.
3. Tear the tofu into rough, craggy bite-size pieces -- torn, not cut, so the ragged edges crisp like shredded chicken. Spread on a clean towel, set a flat pan on top, and press for about 10 minutes to drive out water.
4. Dust the pressed tofu to coat in the starch. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium-high and fry the tofu, turning every couple of minutes, for about 6 minutes until deep golden and brittle all over; lift out onto a plate.
5. Lower the heat to medium and soften the sauce onion in the same pan with its residual oil, stirring often, for about 8 minutes until limp and browning at the edges. Add the garlic for a minute until fragrant, then the paprika, cumin, oregano and chili -- stir just 10-15 seconds, only until they bloom, or they scorch and turn bitter. Pour in the tomatoes, smoked sriracha and sugar; simmer about 10 minutes until thick and brick-red.
6. Fold the crisped tofu back in and simmer just 5 minutes to coat -- no longer, or the crust softens into mush. Squeeze over the lime juice, spoon over the rice, and pile on the drained pickled onion.

## Notes

Tinga is normally shredded chicken braised in a smoky chipotle-tomato sauce. Torn firm tofu stands in convincingly: pressing drives out water so it can crisp, a manioc-starch crust sets a brittle shell that survives the sauce, and the ragged torn edges read as pulled meat in a way cubes never do. Keep the final simmer short -- the crisp crust is the whole point and prolonged braising waterlogs it back to soft.

With no chipotle in adobo, the smoke is built from three sources: smoked sriracha (smoke plus the vinegary adobo tang), sweet paprika, and dried chili. Bloom the ground spices for only seconds -- past about 150C they scorch and the smoky volatiles turn acrid, and that bitterness is locked in. The pinch of sugar balances the tomato's acidity the way chipotle's natural sweetness would.

The pickled onion is not a garnish afterthought: raw red onion's sharp bite comes from sulfur compounds that acid only breaks down slowly, so it needs the full 15-30 minute soak to mellow. Start it first and it doubles as the bright, sharp counterpoint to the rich sauce.
