# Blueberry-Raspberry Kissel with Whipped Tofu Cream

**Time:** 15 min active / 20 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ------------ | -------- | -------- | ------ |
| Silken Tofu | 200g | 1/2 block | 124 |
| Skyr | 50g | 3 1/2 tbsp | 26 |
| Almond Butter | 15g | 2 3/4 tsp | 92 |
| Lime | 15g | 1/4 lime | 4 |
| Vanilla Extract | 1g | 1/4 tsp | 3 |
| Salt |  | to taste | 0 |
| Pumpkin Seeds | 15g | 2 tbsp | 84 |
| Hemp Seeds | 10g | 1 tbsp | 55 |
| Frozen Blueberries | 100g | 2/3 cup | 51 |
| Frozen Raspberries | 90g | 3/4 cup | 47 |
| Maple Syrup | 16g | 2 1/2 tsp | 42 |
| Arrowroot Powder | 4g | 1 1/2 tsp | 14 |

**Total:** 542 kcal \| 31g protein

## Steps

1. Set the silken tofu in a fine sieve over a bowl and let it drain, about 10 minutes -- don't press, just let gravity pull the water out; this is what keeps the cream from weeping later.
2. Blend the drained tofu with the skyr, almond butter, lime zest, vanilla, and a pinch of salt until completely smooth and glossy, about 1-2 minutes, scraping down once or twice; chill while you make everything else.
3. Toast the pumpkin and hemp seeds in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, for 3-4 minutes until they pop and smell nutty; tip onto a plate and salt lightly.
4. Simmer the blueberries and raspberries with the maple over medium heat, 3-4 minutes until thawed and collapsing into a loose juice; whisk the arrowroot with a splash of cold water, stir it in, and cook just 20-30 seconds until glossy and lightly thickened -- no longer, or it thins back out. Off the heat, stir in the lime juice and let it cool to just warm.
5. Spoon the cold cream into bowls, pour the warm kissel over so it pools and streaks, and scatter with the toasted seeds.

## Notes

A kissel is fruit set just to the edge of holding its shape with a little starch -- looser than jam, glossier than compote. Arrowroot is the right thickener here: tart berries are acidic enough to thin a root starch, so the slurry goes in at the very end, cooks only seconds, and the lime is stirred in off the heat. Boil it longer or add the acid early and the set slackens back to syrup. Pouring it warm over the cold cream is the whole point -- the two temperatures meet on the spoon. The cream is whipped silken tofu, which blends to something between mousse and yogurt and carries the protein; draining it first and leaning on vanilla, lime, and a little almond butter keeps any beany note away. It's tangy and barely sweet on purpose -- the kissel brings the sweetness.
