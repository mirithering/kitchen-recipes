# Soy-Oat Porridge with Rhubarb-Ginger Compote

**Time:** 20 min active / 25 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ------------ | -------- | -------- | ------ |
| Soy Flakes | 45g | 7 1/2 tbsp | 173 |
| Water | 150g | 10 1/4 tbsp | 0 |
| Salt | 1g | pinch | 0 |
| Rhubarb | 100g | 3/4 cup diced | 21 |
| Honey | 12g | 1 3/4 tsp | 36 |
| Fresh Ginger | 5g | 1 tsp grated | 4 |
| Rolled Oats | 45g | 8 3/4 tbsp | 171 |
| Pumpkin Seeds | 18g | 2 1/4 tbsp | 101 |
| Soy Milk | 200g | 3/4 cup | 82 |
| Cinnamon | 0.5g | 1/4 tsp | 1 |

**Total:** 589 kcal \| 35g protein

## Steps

1. Bring the soy flakes, water, and salt to a boil in a small pot, then drop to a low simmer and cook, stirring now and then, for about 4 minutes -- the denser soy flakes need a head start before the oats go in.
2. Meanwhile, combine the rhubarb and honey with a splash of water in a second small pot; simmer gently, covered, for 5-6 minutes until the pieces are just tender but still hold their shape, then stir in the grated ginger off the heat so it stays bright and pungent.
3. Stir the rolled oats into the soy-flake pot and simmer, stirring only occasionally, for 6-7 minutes until thick and creamy -- a light hand keeps it silky rather than gluey.
4. Toast the pumpkin seeds in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, for about 2 minutes until they puff and start to pop.
5. Off the heat, stir the soy milk and cinnamon into the porridge to loosen it to a creamy, spoonable consistency. Spoon into a bowl, top with the rhubarb-ginger compote, and scatter the toasted pumpkin seeds over.

## Notes

Soy flakes carry the protein here but can't make a porridge alone -- on their own they stay chewy and turn gluey when pushed, so they cap at half the dry grain and the oats do the creamy work. Giving the soy a few minutes' head start lets it soften fully (it cooks slower than oats) without overcooking the oats into paste. Holding the soy milk until the very end keeps it from scorching on the pot bottom, where plant milks catch easily.

The compote stays a compote, not jam: a short covered simmer tenderizes the rhubarb while the pieces still hold, and the ginger goes in off the heat so its heat doesn't cook out. Rhubarb's tartness is the point -- just enough honey to round it, leaning on acid rather than sweetness.
