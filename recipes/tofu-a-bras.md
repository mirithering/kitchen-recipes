# Tofu a Bras

**Time:** 25 min active / 35 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ----------------- | -------- | --------------- | ------ |
| Firm Tofu | 150g | 3/4 block | 128 |
| Potato | 180g | 1 cup diced | 139 |
| Olive Oil | 10g | 3/4 tbsp | 88 |
| Onion | 110g | 1 medium | 44 |
| Olive Oil | 5g | 1 tsp | 44 |
| Garlic | 6g | 2 cloves | 9 |
| Salt | 2g | 1/3 tsp | 0 |
| Black Pepper | 1g | to taste | 0 |
| Egg | 100g | 2 large | 143 |
| Kalamata Olives | 18g | 6 olives | 21 |
| Basil | 10g | 1 handful | 2 |
| Lemon | 7g | 1 wedge | 2 |

**Total:** 620 kcal \| 35g protein

## Steps

1. Wrap the tofu in a clean towel, set a heavy pan or a couple of cans on top, and press for about 15 minutes until it stops weeping and feels firm and dry.
2. Cut the potato into thin matchstick batons. Rinse briefly to shed loose starch, then parboil in water for 3-4 minutes, just until the edges yield but the centers stay firm; drain and pat thoroughly dry -- wet potato steams instead of crisping.
3. Heat the larger amount of olive oil in a wide pan over medium heat. Add the potato in a single layer and fry, tossing only every couple of minutes, for 8-10 minutes until pale gold, then raise the heat and fry 3-4 minutes more until deep golden and crackly-crisp. Lift out with a slotted spoon and set aside.
4. Slice the onion thin. In the same pan with the remaining oil, fry over medium heat, stirring often, for 8-10 minutes until soft and golden; add the sliced garlic for the last minute until fragrant but not browned.
5. Tear the pressed tofu into ragged bite-size shards. Push the onion aside, lay the tofu in the pan, and fry undisturbed for 4-5 minutes until the undersides are golden and release cleanly, then toss to crisp all over. Season with salt and pepper.
6. Fold the crisp potato back through the pan. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt and pepper, then pull the pan fully OFF the heat and pour them in. Fold gently and constantly for 30-60 seconds -- the residual heat sets them into a glossy, barely-there custard; stop while they still look a touch underdone.
7. Slide onto the plate at once. Scatter with chopped olives and torn basil, add a squeeze of lemon, and finish with black pepper.

## Notes

Bacalhau a Bras is Lisbon comfort food -- salt cod tangled with crisp potato straws, sweet onion, and eggs barely set into a creamy bind. Pressed firm tofu stands in for the cod: driving out its water lets the torn shards crisp at the edges and drink up the egg the way flaked fish does. This version trades the usual sweet-potato shortcut for real potato, done right -- a home pan can't crisp raw straws (at matchstick thinness they only steam), so the potato is parboiled just to the firm-centered stage, dried hard, then fried gently to build a starch crust before a blast of high heat makes it crackle. That parcooking is also what keeps it from collapsing to mush when folded through at the end.

The egg is everything. Pull the pan fully off the heat before it goes in -- in a mixture loosened with oil and potato, egg protein sets around 80C, and a few seconds too long on direct heat tips it into dry, weeping curds. Folded into residual warmth it stays glossy and custardy; plate it while it still looks a touch underdone, because carryover heat finishes the set on the way to the table. Briny olives, torn basil, and a squeeze of lemon cut the richness.
