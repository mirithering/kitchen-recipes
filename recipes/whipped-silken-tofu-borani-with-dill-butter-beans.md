# Whipped Silken Tofu Borani with Dill Butter Beans

**Time:** 20 min active / 60 min total

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Kcal |
| ------------ | -------- | ------------ | ------ |
| Silken Tofu | 200g | drained | 124 |
| Lemon Juice | 10g | 2 tsp | 2 |
| Dried Dill | 1.5g | 1 1/2 tsp | 4 |
| Salt | 1g | pinch | 0 |
| Pumpkin Seeds | 20g | 2 1/2 tbsp | 112 |
| Olive Oil | 8g | 1/2 tbsp | 71 |
| Garlic | 3g | 1 clove | 4 |
| Dried Dill | 1.5g | 1 1/2 tsp | 4 |
| Butter Beans | 240g | 1 can | 190 |
| Olive Oil | 7g | 1/2 tbsp | 62 |
| Sumac | 1.5g | pinch | 4 |
| Cayenne Pepper | 0.3g | pinch | 1 |

**Total:** 578 kcal | 33g protein

## Steps

1. Blend the silken tofu with the lemon juice, salt, and dried dill until completely smooth, about 30 seconds on high -- chase out every lump so it turns glossy and pourable, never chalky. Chill at least 1 hour so the dried dill softens and blooms through the cream.
2. Toast the pumpkin seeds in a dry pan over medium-high heat, shaking constantly, until they pop and swell to an even almond-brown, 3 to 4 minutes; tip them onto a cold plate the moment the popping slows, before the pan's heat scorches them bitter.
3. Warm the garlic in the olive oil in a small pan over low heat until fragrant and barely golden, about 2 minutes, keeping it gentle so it never browns hard. Add the butter beans with a splash of their canning liquid and the dried dill, and warm slowly without ever boiling, about 7 minutes, pressing a few beans against the pan to thicken the juices into a silky sauce.
4. Spread the chilled borani across a wide plate, spoon the warm beans and their dill oil into the center, drizzle with the cold olive oil, scatter the pumpkin seeds, and dust the plate with sumac and a whisper of cayenne.

## Notes

Borani is the Persian-Turkish family of dishes where a vegetable is folded into thick garlicky yogurt; here whipped silken tofu stands in for the yogurt. Blended hard, it takes on the body of strained labneh but a clean, milky background that lets the dill lead. The long chill is not idle time -- dried dill is roughly three times as strong as fresh and needs moisture and an hour to wake up. Blended into the cold cream it rehydrates from the inside and tastes herbal rather than dusty. Dill is fragile in heat, so in the beans it goes in with their liquid, never into bare hot oil, which would turn it grassy and bitter. The beans are already cooked and only want a low, slow warming -- a boil splits their skins. Sumac's fruity tartness does the final brightening, so the cream needs only a little lemon, and the garlic is softened in oil rather than left raw and sharp.
